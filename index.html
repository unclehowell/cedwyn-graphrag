How this is built

Propositions. Every statement carried by two or more distinct sources, and every evidence-catalogue record, is rewritten as one neutral sentence naming who did what, when, and the record that shows it. Sentences that mean the same thing are merged (same record or same event, near-identical wording). Each proposition is weighed by the number of distinct primary sources behind it: a court judgment, a Land Registry title or letter, a dated press item, an official or archive record, or a catalogued evidence record. It is flagged primary when at least one such record backs it. Estate pages that copy a point add nothing to its size; they are listed under the proposition as estate pages. The rewriting was done by a language model and is not checked line by line: read the source before relying on a proposition.

Statements. Each source is one Cedwyn webapp repository, the central evidence catalogue, or the legal wiki. A statement copied into many pages of one source counts once for that source. Page furniture and text repeated on most pages of a source are set aside as noise. Statements that carry personal or contact details are left out. Nothing here is a finding.
